Adaptive Shared Control for Collaborative Robots
via Vision-Based Motion Prediction and Ergonomic
Evaluation
Abstract—As human-robot collaboration (HRC) becomes increasingly prevalent in dynamic environments, seamlessly blending human intent with autonomous robotic execution remains
a critical challenge. Standard motion prediction models often
exhibit overconfidence or fail to account for human ergonomic
comfort, leading to jerky robotic responses and operator fatigue.
This paper proposes a novel adaptive shared control framework designed for collaborative robot arms. By integrating a
deep learning-based motion predictor with an adaptive weight
generator, the system explicitly fuses the reliability of algorithmic predictions with real-time human arm comfort scores.
A trajectory smoother dynamically allocates control authority,
ensuring stable and fluent cooperation. The proposed control
loop, implemented via a quaternion-based inverse-kinematics
formulation and an optimal LQR controller, strictly constrains
absolute joint positions to ensure robust tracking. Experimental
validation on a physical collaborative robot demonstrates significant improvements in task fluency, reduced control effort, and
enhanced human postural comfort compared to traditional fixedauthority baselines.
Index Terms—Human-Robot Collaboration, Shared Control,
Deep Learning, Motion Prediction, Quaternions.

I. I NTRODUCTION

T

HE The integration of collaborative robots (cobots) into
shared workspaces requires advanced control paradigms
that can seamlessly adapt to human behavior. While visionbased skeleton tracking and deep learning (DL) models
have significantly improved human motion prediction, relying
solely on autonomous predictions can be dangerous if the
algorithm’s confidence drops due to occlusion or sudden
movements.
Existing literature often overlooks the necessity of dynamically blending robot autonomy with human ergonomic
requirements. To address this gap, this paper introduces an
adaptive shared control architecture. The key contributions of
this work are threefold:
1) A novel adaptive weight generation mechanism that
fuses DL prediction reliability with real-time arm comfort scores mapped against an optimal human pose
dataset.
2) A robust trajectory smoothing filter that ensures stable
control authority blending without inducing oscillatory
behavior in the cobot.
3) A rigorously constrained, quaternion-based control formulation validated on a physical robot platform, demonstrating statistical superiority over fixed-weight blending
methods.

II. R ELATED W ORK
A. Human Motion Prediction in HRC
Recent advancements in vision-based systems have enabled
real-time skeleton tracking. However, standard DL approaches
often lack confidence bounds, making them susceptible to
anomalous predictions during dynamic tasks.
B. Authority Blending and Shared Control
Traditional shared control systems typically utilize static authority weights or rely on simplistic distance-based heuristics.
Dynamic weight allocation that incorporates human physiological states remains under-explored.
III. S YSTEM A RCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
The proposed control framework processes inputs from an
AI-enabled vision system to extract the human wrist position
pwr and human joint angles θhu . A DL-based motion prediction module estimates the future trajectory p̂pre and its
associated error ϵ.
Simultaneously, the Adaptive Weight Generator evaluates
algorithmic reliability and arm comfort, synthesizing a blending weight w. The Trajectory Smoother then fuses the predicted trajectory with the current robot feedback pf b to generate a smooth command psm , which is subsequently tracked
by the robot via an optimal LQR controller.
IV. P ROPOSED M ETHODOLOGY
A. Vision-based Motion Prediction
The motion prediction module utilizes a recurrent neural
network architecture to process sequential skeleton data. Let
pwr (t) be the actual wrist position captured by the camera at
time t, and p̂pre (t) be the model’s prediction.
B. Adaptive Weight Generator
The core novelty of the framework lies in the dynamic
generation of the blending weight w, derived from two distinct
metrics.
First, the prediction reliability is quantified by the instantaneous prediction error:
ϵ(t) = ∥p̂pre (t) − pwr (t)∥2

(1)

The corresponding reliability score sr (t) is defined using an
exponential decay function:
sr (t) = exp(−λϵ(t)),

λ>0

(2)

2

where λ governs the sensitivity to prediction errors.
Second, the arm comfort score sj is computed by comparing
the current human joint configuration θhu against an optimal
pose dataset θopt to minimize biomechanical stress.
The final dynamic weight w is a linear fusion of these two
scores:
w = α · sr + β · sj
(3)
where α and β are tunable hyperparameters reflecting the
relative importance of algorithmic confidence and human
ergonomics.
C. Trajectory Smoothing and Shared Control
To prevent discontinuous commands from reaching the
robot controller, authority blending is formulated as a discretetime smoothing filter for a future horizon step k:
psm (t + k) = w · p̂pre (t + k) + (1 − w) · pf b

(4)

This formulation guarantees asymptotic stability during the
transition of control authority.
D. Robust Robot Control and Kinematics
To resolve the inverse kinematics (IK) from the smoothed
trajectory psm to the reference joint configuration qref , this
framework employs a pseudo-inverse Jacobian method. Crucially, end-effector orientation is represented strictly using unit
quaternions rather than Euler angles. This eliminates representation singularities and ensures smooth rotational tracking
during complex manipulative tasks.
Furthermore, to guarantee robustness in the numerical integration step, the IK algorithm is designed to directly constrain
the final resulting joint angle vector q against mechanical
limits, rather than solely bounding the differential change
∆q. The resulting reference qref is then tracked by an LQR
controller to compute optimal joint velocities q̇cur .
V. E XPERIMENTAL S ETUP
The proposed architecture was validated using a highfidelity setup featuring an AI-capable camera for skeleton
tracking and a physical collaborative robot arm. The software
backend was developed using cross-platform C/C++ build
systems managed via CMake to ensure real-time execution.
VI. R ESULTS AND D ISCUSSION
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed framework,
ablation studies were conducted against two baselines:
1) Fixed weight blending (w = const).
2) Reliability-only blending (ignoring comfort score sj ).
Results indicate that the proposed adaptive weight generator
significantly reduces the root-mean-square error (RMSE) of
the trajectory tracking while minimizing sharp torque gradients
at the robot joints. Furthermore, analysis of the operator’s
posture demonstrated a measurable improvement in overall
arm comfort over extended operational periods.

VII. C ONCLUSION
This paper presented an adaptive shared control framework
for human-robot collaboration that uniquely fuses deep learning prediction reliability with human ergonomic comfort. By
ensuring mathematically stable authority blending and enforcing strict quaternion-based kinematic constraints, the proposed
system yields safer, more fluent collaborative performance.
Future work will investigate the integration of haptic feedback
and the exploration of multi-agent collaborative environments.
